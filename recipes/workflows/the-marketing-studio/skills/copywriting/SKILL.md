# Copywriting for Marketing

Transforms creative briefs into compelling copy across all marketing touchpoints. Use this skill when a campaign brief from The Strategist needs to become copy-ready text for specific deliverables.

## Copy Frameworks

Choose the framework that best fits the campaign narrative framework and emotional tone defined by The Strategist.

### AIDA (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action)
The classic persuasion sequence. Best for campaigns where the audience needs to move through a complete decision journey.

| Stage | Purpose | Example |
| :---- | :------ | :------ |
| **Attention** | Stop the scroll. A bold headline, provocative question, or surprising stat. | "Your morning coffee is hiding a secret." |
| **Interest** | Build curiosity. Add detail that makes the audience want to learn more. | "Most blends use beans roasted months ago. Ours are packed within 72 hours of harvest." |
| **Desire** | Paint the benefit. Show the transformation or emotional payoff. | "Imagine that first sip — rich, bold, knowing every bean was picked at peak ripeness." |
| **Action** | One clear instruction. No ambiguity. | "Taste the difference. Subscribe now." |

*Use when:* launching new products, re-engaging cold audiences, high-consideration purchases

### PAS (Problem → Agitate → Solve)
Best for pain-point-driven campaigns (Problem-Solution narrative framework).

| Stage | Purpose | Example |
| :---- | :------ | :------ |
| **Problem** | Name the frustration your audience feels. | "Stale coffee. Bland mornings. Another day you drag yourself out of bed." |
| **Agitate** | Make the pain vivid. Amplify the cost of NOT solving it. | "You're settling for mediocre when every morning could feel like a weekend at your favorite cafe." |
| **Solve** | Present your product as the inevitable answer. | "Ethica Brew delivers fresh-roasted, single-origin beans to your door within a week of harvest. No subscriptions traps. Just great coffee." |

*Use when:* productivity tools, cleaning products, financial services, B2B SaaS, any utility-driven product

### 4U (Urgent → Unique → Useful → Ultra-specific)
Best for headlines and short-form copy. Every headline should pass the 4U test.

| Criterion | Question to Ask | Example |
| :-------- | :-------------- | :------ |
| **Urgent** | Why must they read/act now? | "Last chance for 20% off" vs "Sale ends midnight" |
| **Unique** | What makes this different from every competitor? | "The only water bottle made from ocean-bound plastic" |
| **Useful** | What do they gain? Be specific. | "Save 47 hours a month with automated reporting" |
| **Ultra-specific** | Numbers, names, concrete details. | "3,427 entrepreneurs used this template last week" |

*Use when:* headlines, subject lines, social captions, banner copy

### Before-After-Bridge (BAB)
Short-form persuasion. State the current reality, the desired reality, and how you bridge them.

```
Before: "You're spending 3 hours a week on manual data entry."
After:  "Imagine dashboards that update themselves."
Bridge: "Our AI connector syncs your tools in one click."
```

*Use when:* social ads, short-form video scripts, landing page hero sections

### Feature → Benefit → Emotion (FBE)
Connect product specs to human feelings. Never list a feature without its benefit and emotional payoff.

| Layer | Question | Example |
| :---- | :------- | :------ |
| **Feature** | What does it do? | "40-hour battery life" |
| **Benefit** | What does that mean for the user? | "Never charge your headphones on a trip" |
| **Emotion** | How does that make them feel? | "Total freedom — you're in the zone, not hunting for an outlet" |

*Use when:* product descriptions, feature call-outs, comparison tables

## Tone & Voice

### Brand Voice Dimensions

Define the brand's position on these five spectrums:

| Dimension | −2 | −1 | 0 | +1 | +2 |
| :-------- | :- | :- | :- | :- | :- |
| **Formality** | Slang, fragments | Casual conversation | Neutral professional | Polished business | Formal, academic |
| **Energy** | Quiet, understated | Calm, steady | Balanced | Energetic, lively | Loud, urgent |
| **Humor** | Dead serious | Professional | Light warmth | Witty, clever | Irreverent, funny |
| **Empathy** | Transactional | Efficient | Understanding | Warm, caring | Deeply personal |
| **Confidence** | Humble, questioning | Collaborative | Assertive | Authoritative | Bold, provocative |

### How to Use the Dimensions

1. **Given brand voice from the creative brief** → locate on each spectrum
2. **If no brand voice is provided** → derive from the Emotional Tone in the creative brief:
   - Luxury → Formality +1, Energy −1, Humor −2, Empathy 0, Confidence +2
   - Playful → Formality −2, Energy +2, Humor +2, Empathy +1, Confidence 0
   - Trust (banking/health) → Formality +1, Energy 0, Humor −1, Empathy +1, Confidence +1
   - Innovation/Tech → Formality −1, Energy +1, Humor 0, Empathy 0, Confidence +1
3. **Document the voice profile** as part of the copy deliverables so downstream agents (Art Director) can align visual tone with verbal tone

### Voice Profile Example

```yaml
voice_profile:
  formality: 0     # neutral professional
  energy: 1        # energetic
  humor: 1         # witty
  empathy: 1       # warm
  confidence: 1    # authoritative
  summary: "Confident and energetic but approachable. We sound like an expert friend who's genuinely excited to help."
```

## CTA Formulas

### CTA Anatomy

Every CTA has three components:

| Component | Purpose | Example |
| :-------- | :------ | :------ |
| **Verb** | The action you want them to take | "Shop", "Learn", "Start", "Try", "Get" |
| **Value** | The benefit they receive | "Your Free Trial", "The Look", "20% Off" |
| **Urgency** | Why now (optional, use sparingly) | "Today Only", "Limited Edition" |

### CTA Archetypes

| Archetype | Formula | Examples | Best For |
| :-------- | :------ | :------- | :------- |
| Direct | `[Verb] [Value]` | "Shop the Sale", "Get Started Free" | Primary buttons, hero sections |
| Curiosity Gap | `[Verb] [Teaser]` | "See What's Inside", "Discover Your Match" | Social ads, email subject lines |
| Social Proof | `[Verb] [Number] [Others]` | "Join 50,000+ Creators", "See Why 9/10 Recommend" | Landing pages, testimonials |
| Risk Reversal | `[Verb] [Risk-Free Promise]` | "Try Risk-Free for 30 Days", "Cancel Anytime" | High-consideration products |
| Personal | `[Verb] [Personal Outcome]` | "Build Your Dream Setup", "Find Your Fit" | Lifestyle, fashion, customization |
| Scarcity | `[Time] [Verb] [Value]` | "Last Chance — Shop the Drop", "Only 24 Hours Left" | Flash sales, limited drops |
| Command | `[Imperative Verb] [Object]` | "Fuel Your Adventure", "Own the Night" | Bold/adrenaline brands |

### CTA Testing Priorities

When writing multiple CTA variants, prioritize testing:

1. **Verb choice** — "Try" vs "Start" vs "Get" can change conversion by 30%+
2. **First vs second person** — "Start Your Free Trial" vs "Start My Free Trial"
3. **Length** — 2-4 words is optimal; 5+ words drops click-through
4. **Urgency presence** — Add only if the offer genuinely has a time limit

## Per-Channel Copy Guidelines

### Landing Page

| Section | Length | Purpose |
| :------ | :----- | :------ |
| Hero Headline | 5-12 words | State the single most important value proposition |
| Hero Subheadline | 10-25 words | Add specificity and support the headline |
| Benefit Sections (3-4) | 50-100 words each | One key benefit per section with supporting detail |
| CTA Button | 2-4 words | Action + Value (e.g., "Start Free Trial") |
| Trust Signals | 10-30 words | Testimonial, stat, or guarantee |

### Email Marketing

| Element | Length | Purpose |
| :------ | :----- | :------ |
| Subject Line | 30-50 characters | Stop the scroll; create curiosity or urgency |
| Preview Text | 50-100 characters | Support the subject line; add a second hook |
| Headline | 5-10 words | Restate or expand the subject line promise |
| Body Copy | 50-150 words | Tell the story, deliver the value |
| CTA Button | 2-4 words | One clear action |
| P.S. | 10-20 words | Last-chance urgency or secondary CTA |

### Social Media (Carousel / Single Image)

| Element | Length | Purpose |
| :------ | :----- | :------ |
| Image Overlay Text | 3-8 words | Hero message visible without tapping |
| Caption Hook (first line) | 5-15 words | Stop the scroll; 80% of readers never get past this |
| Caption Body | 40-125 words | Story, value, context |
| CTA | 2-4 words | "Link in bio", "Tap to shop", "Drop a comment" |

### Poster / Billboard

| Element | Length | Purpose |
| :------ | :----- | :------ |
| Headline | 3-8 words | Must be readable in 3 seconds from a moving car |
| Subheadline | 5-10 words (optional) | Add context only if essential |
| Logo / Brand | Visible but secondary | Recognition, not reading |
| URL / QR | Minimal | Trackable action |

### Video Ad (15-30 seconds)

| Section | Time | Purpose |
| :------ | :--- | :------ |
| Hook | 0-3s | Stop the scroll — pattern interrupt |
| Problem/Setup | 3-8s | Relatable context |
| Solution/Product | 8-18s | Demonstration or benefit |
| CTA | 18-30s | Clear action with urgency |

## Copy Output Format

Each copy deliverable must include:

```markdown
## Copy: [Asset name / File name]

**Voice Profile:**
```yaml
formality: 0
energy: 1
humor: 0
empathy: 1
confidence: 1
```

**Copy Blocks:**
- **Headline:** [Primary headline text]
- **Subheadline:** [Supporting text]
- **Body:** [Full body copy if applicable]
- **CTA:** [Button/link text]
- **Alt text / Overlay:** [Text that appears on image]

**Variants:**
- v1: [Headline A] | [CTA A]
- v2: [Headline B] | [CTA B]
- v3: [Headline C] | [CTA C]

**Notes:** [Rationale, tone justification, brand voice alignment]
```

## Common Copy Mistakes

- **Feature dumping** — Listing features without benefits. Every feature must answer "so what?"
- **Weak verbs** — "We help people get things done" → "We automate your busywork"
- **Jargon overload** — "Leverage our omnichannel synergy" → plain language wins every time
- **No hierarchy** — The headline tries to say everything. One message per piece.
- **CTA confusion** — Multiple competing actions on one asset. One primary CTA per customer touchpoint.
- **Tone inconsistency** — Playful headline, formal body copy. The voice profile must be consistent throughout.
- **Missing the audience** — Copy that describes what the brand does rather than what the customer needs.
